LaReg Corp.

Summer 2026

Regulatory Research Intern, Boston

State utility commissions keep dockets, the case files for proposed energy projects like solar farms and transmission lines. Companies file PDFs into them, and LaReg sells the structured data buried in those PDFs. I rebuilt the pipeline that gets it out. Given a docket, it finds the filings in Postgres, pulls the PDFs from S3, runs OCR with AWS Textract on scanned pages, and sends the text to Gemini to fill in a structured record where every field cites the page it came from.

The field I spent the most time on was ownership. The model kept naming the applicant as the owner, so I added deterministic checks after extraction that blank the answer unless the cited text actually says who owns the project. I also retrained the BERT classifier that sorts each project into a subcategory.

The other half of the job was how the work got done. I ran several AI coding agents at once, each on its own piece of the pipeline, inside guardrails I set: read-only access to production data, changes to outputs could only add and never overwrite, and every claim had to cite a docket number. My part was scoping the tasks, reviewing what came back, and deciding what shipped.

Python, Gemini, AWS S3, Textract, PostgreSQL, PyTorch, Hugging Face
